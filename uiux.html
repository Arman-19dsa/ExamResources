① UI vs UX
UI — User Interface
  • Visual elements: buttons, icons, colors, typography, layouts
  • How the product looks
  • Concerns individual screens & elements
  • Subset of UX
UX — User Experience
  • Overall feeling, ease, satisfaction of the journey
  • How the product works & feels
  • Covers end-to-end user journey
  • Encompasses UI

Example: Banking app — beautiful login screen = good UI. 10-step transfer = bad UX.

Interview vs Survey
Interviews ✓
  • Deep qualitative insights (Why?)
  • Follow-up probing questions
  • Small sample, high quality
Surveys ✓
  • Fast & scalable (100s of users)
  • Quantifiable data patterns
  • Low cost, no scheduling

③ Visual Design Principles
HierarchySize, contrast, weight to show importance · "Emergency" > "View History"
ConsistencySame buttons, colors, fonts across all screens & modules
FeedbackLoading spinners, confirmations, error messages after every action
AccessibilityWCAG contrast ratios, labels, alt text, avoid color-only cues
TypographyFont hierarchy: Heading → Sub → Body → Caption; max 2–3 families
ColorPrimary + Secondary + Accent palette; harmony & contrast (4.5:1 min)
Spacing8-pt grid; whitespace creates grouping & breathing room
IconographyUniform style (flat/outlined); label icons for clarity; avoid ambiguity
Design Systems
Atomic DesignAtoms → Molecules → Organisms → Templates → Pages
Material DesignGoogle's system: elevation, motion, grid, components

Design systems ensure consistency & speed across teams. Shared components = no module-by-module redesign.

Why Wireframe? (7 Benefits)
  • Defines layout & information hierarchy
  • Identifies UX problems early
  • Saves dev time & cost
  • Facilitates team collaboration
  • Stakeholder approval before build
  • Clarifies navigation structure

    ④ Nielsen's 10 Usability Heuristics
    1
    Visibility of System StatusAlways inform users what's happening — loading spinner, progress bar
    2
    Match Real WorldUse familiar language & concepts, not system jargon
    3
    User Control & FreedomClear undo/redo, "Cancel" options — emergency exits
    4
    Consistency & StandardsSame words/actions mean same thing everywhere
    5
    Error PreventionDesign to avoid problems before they occur
    6
    Recognition not RecallVisible options > memorizing commands
    7
    Flexibility & EfficiencyShortcuts for expert users; adaptable for novices
    8
    Aesthetic & MinimalIrrelevant information competes with relevant ones
    9
    Error RecoveryPlain-language errors + constructive fix suggestions
    10
    Help & DocumentationEasy-to-search support when needed
    Heuristic Evaluation Process

    3–5 UX experts · No real users · Map issues to heuristic · Severity 0–4 · Quick & low-cost

    Severity: 0=no issue · 1=cosmetic · 2=minor · 3=major · 4=catastrophic
