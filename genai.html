Unit I — Introduction to Gen AI

What is Generative AI?

  • Definition – AI that creates new content (text, images, code, audio, video) from patterns in training data
  • Discriminative AI – Classifies existing data (e.g., spam filter)
  • Generative AI – Creates new data matching training distribution
  • Foundation Models – Large pre-trained models (GPT-4, Gemini, Claude)
  • LLMs – Large Language Models; predict next token; billions of params
  • Prompt – Input text/instruction to a GenAI model
  • Hallucination – AI generating confident but factually wrong outputs

Unit II — Finance & Business

Algorithmic Trading & Forecasting

  • Synthetic Data – GAN-generated stock/market data to train models when real data is scarce
  • Scenario Simulation – Simulate bullish/bearish/volatile markets for strategy testing
  • Forecasting – AI predicts price trends using historical + synthetic patterns
  • Overfitting Risk – Models trained on synthetic data may fail on real market shifts

Fraud Detection & Risk Mgmt

  • Synthetic Fraud Patterns – Generate rare fraud transactions to improve detection model training
  • Anomaly Detection – GenAI flags unusual patterns vs. normal baseline
  • Credit Risk Modeling – Synthetic loan data expands training set for better risk scoring
  • Limitation – Synthetic data may not represent evolving real cyber threats

Customer Service & Chatbots

  • LLM Chatbots – Handle queries 24/7 with NLP; reduce agent workload
  • Limitation – Struggles with complex/multilingual/edge-case queries
  • Ethical Risk – Automated financial advice without human oversight

Unit III — Science & Research

Scientific Discovery

  • Drug Discovery – GenAI generates candidate molecules; simulate molecular interactions (GANs / VAEs)
  • Protein Design – Generate new protein structures (AlphaFold-like)
  • Personalized Medicine – GenAI designs drugs based on patient-specific genomics
  • Hypothesis Gen – AI proposes multiple hypotheses from observed patterns

Simulation & Data Augmentation

  • Climate Simulation – Synthetic climate data for long-term scenario modeling
  • Medical Imaging – GAN-generated synthetic MRI scans augment rare-disease datasets
  • Autonomous Vehicles – Generate diverse driving scenarios for safer training
  • Crop / Agri Data – Synthetic datasets for yield prediction in data-scarce settings
  • Population Modeling – Simulate population growth & disease spread scenarios
  • Missing Data – GenAI fills gaps in incomplete datasets

Collaboration & Knowledge Sharing

  • AI Tools – ChatGPT Notion AI Grammarly for report co-creation
  • Multilingual Summaries – Summarize papers in multiple languages
  • Knowledge Distillation – Condense large research corpus into structured knowledge
  • Interdisciplinary Collab – AI bridges domains (bio + CS + physics)

Ethical Implications in Science

  • Academic Integrity – AI-drafted papers; plagiarism & authorship concerns
  • Validation – Synthetic results must be clinically/experimentally verified
  • Reproducibility – AI-generated data may not be reproducible
  • Data Bias – Synthetic data amplifying existing dataset biases
Unit IV — Responsible AI

Ethical Principles of AI

🔍 Transparency Explainable outputs & decisions
⚖️ Fairness No discrimination; equal treatment
🛡️ Privacy Data protection; consent; GDPR
✅ Accountability Clear responsibility for AI actions
🔒 Safety Robustness; prevent misuse

Types of Bias in AI

  • Historical Bias – Training data reflects past discrimination (e.g., hiring mostly males)
  • Representation Bias – Underrepresented groups in dataset
  • Measurement Bias – Faulty data collection instruments/methods
  • Aggregation Bias – One model for all groups with different characteristics
  • Deployment Bias – Model used in context different from training

Bias Mitigation Techniques

StageTechnique
Pre-processingResampling, re-weighting, data augmentation
In-processingFairness constraints in model training
Post-processingCalibrate outputs; adjust thresholds per group
ToolkitsIBM AI Fairness 360 (AIF360); What-If Tool

Stakeholder Roles

  • Developers – Build fair, tested, documented models
  • Organizations – Define ethical policies; audit AI systems
  • Regulators – EU AI Act, GDPR; enforce compliance
  • Users – Report bias; demand explainability
  • Human-in-Loop – Expert review before critical AI decisions (healthcare, law)

IBM AI Fairness 360 (AIF360)

  • Open-source toolkit by IBM for detecting & mitigating bias
  • Provides 70+ fairness metrics & 10+ mitigation algorithms
  • Supports pre/in/post-processing; works with sklearn & PyTorch
  • Use: evaluate disparate impact, equalized odds, calibration
